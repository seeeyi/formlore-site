Scary Masterpieces · 13 works

Death

In these thirteen works Death is rarely a villain. He is a dancer, a debt collector, a rider on a starved horse, a skeleton in a self-portrait. The memento mori tradition, a reminder that everything ends, runs from Burgkmair's Venice in 1510 to Posada's skeletons on the Mexico City streetcar.

Many of them are small prints, and the best of them are very funny in a dry way.

  • Lovers Surprised by DeathHans Burgkmair, 1510
  • Dance of Death: The MiserHans Holbein the Younger, c. 1526
  • Death and the MiserHieronymus Bosch, c. 1485/1490
  • The Four HorsemenAlbrecht Dürer, 1498
  • Knight, Death, and the DevilAlbrecht Dürer, 1513
  • The SkeletonsGiovanni Battista Piranesi, 1745–50
  • Memento Mori, “To This Favour”William Michael Harnett, 1879
  • Dance of Death: Death the StranglerAlfred Rethel, 1850
  • My Portrait in the Year 1960James Ensor, 1888
  • Night, from On Death, Part IMax Klinger, 1889
  • And His Name That Sat on Him Was DeathOdilon Redon, 1899
  • The Race Track (Death on a Pale Horse)Albert Pinkham Ryder, c. 1896–1908
  • The Skeleton of the BusesJosé Guadalupe Posada, c. 1900–10
