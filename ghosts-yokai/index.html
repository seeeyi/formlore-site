Ghosts & Yōkai

Four Japanese works from the nineteenth century: a kabuki ghost, a demon claiming back his arm, a joyful parade of monsters and a courtesan whose robes show the torments of hell.

Ghost stories were a summer habit in Edo-period Japan, and yōkai (supernatural creatures) were a favourite subject for artists who found them irresistible to draw.

  • Onoe Matsusuke as the Ghost of OiwaUtagawa Toyokuni I, 1812
  • Sketch of Ibaraki-dōjiShibata Zeshin, c. 1840
  • Demons and MonstersTakai Kōzan, ca. 1870s
  • The Hell CourtesanUtagawa Kunisada II, late 1850s
