The Night-Hag Visiting Lapland Witches

Henry Fuseli, 1796

Collection note

Fuseli took his subject from a simile in Milton's Paradise Lost, in which the night-hag rides the air “lured with the smell of infant blood” to dance with the witches of Lapland while the labouring moon eclipses at their charms. A witch crouches over a pale infant as the hag sweeps in above, lit by an eerie moon. It is the painter of The Nightmare at his most theatrical.
